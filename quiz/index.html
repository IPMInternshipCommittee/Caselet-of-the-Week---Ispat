Caselet of the Week

Ispat — The Logistics Lever

This caselet works through a steel manufacturer's logistics cost problem using a simple four-step framework: understand the problem, compare the transportation modes, diagnose the root cause, and build an integrated recommendation.

Not every large cost is a saving. Focus first on separating an observed cost from what can actually be recovered within the existing contractual and labour constraints.

Understand the Problem → Compare the Modes → Diagnose the Root Cause → Recommend
  • What is the engagement really about? Define the objective and the constraints: near-term savings, service levels and contractual commitments.
  • Where is the inefficiency? Identify the largest cost pools and the bottleneck at the port transfer point, and quantify its cost.
  • Why does it happen, and what should Ispat do? Trace the cost back to the KPI that drives it, then design a testable, accountable fix.

ConceptWhat you learnt
Cost Pool AnalysisIdentifying the largest areas of expenditure helps consultants prioritise where to investigate. The largest cost pool should not automatically be treated as the largest source of recoverable savings.
Cost-to-ServeThe cost incurred in delivering a product or service to a customer. It can vary depending on the route, transportation mode, delivery requirements and operational constraints.
Root Cause AnalysisLooking beyond the visible symptom to identify the underlying process, incentive or decision that creates the problem.
System vs. Local OptimisationLocal optimisation improves one team or activity. System optimisation considers the impact on the entire process, including total cost, service levels and downstream consequences.
KPI Design and AccountabilityKPIs influence decisions and behaviour. Well-designed KPIs align individual teams with broader business objectives and assign clear ownership for end-to-end outcomes.
